**408.** End of Match Procedure

**408.1.** A match is complete once a match result is recorded by players or a tournament official.
**408.2.**
**408.2.a.** If the time limit for a competition round is reached before a winner is determined, the
**408.2.b.** player whose turn it is finishes their turn.

**408.2.c.** After that player's turn is complete, three additional turns are played, and then the
**408.2.d.** match ends.
**408.3.**
**408.3.a.** If the game is incomplete at the end of the additional turns, a player is declared the
**408.3.b.** winner of the game if they have a point lead of two or more. If no player has a
**408.4.** point lead of two or more, the game is a draw.

**408.4.a.** Additional turns are untimed, but at professional OPL a judge must be present to
ensure a reasonable pace of play.
**408.4.b.**
**408.4.c.** If the players are between games when the round time ends, no new game is
**408.4.c.1.** started.

**408.4.c.2.** The winner of a match is the first player who reaches the appropriate game win score.
**408.4.c.3.**
If neither player has reached that score by the end of the end of match procedure,
the player with more game wins wins the match.

If players are tied on game wins, the match is a draw.

Single elimination matches use untimed rounds so players can play their games to a natural
conclusion. In the event that single elimination rounds must use a timer, the following
altered End of Match Procedure can be used to determine a winner if time runs out:

When time is reached before a winner is determined, if players are in-between
games, if a player has more game wins in the match they win the match. If game
wins are tied, proceed to 408.4.d.

When time is reached before a winner is determined, if players are in the middle of
a game, the player whose turn it is finishes their turn.

After that player's turn is complete, three additional turns are played. At the
conclusion of those additional turns:

If a player has the most points in the current game, they win that game.
Then, if a player has more game wins in the match, they win the match. If
the game score is tied, proceed to 408.4.d.

If the point score of the current game is tied, but a player has the most
game wins in the match, that player wins the match.

If the point score of the current game is tied and the game score of the
match is tied, players continue to play until the next point is scored. The
player who scores that point wins the match. This does not remove normal
scoring restrictions if the next point is the Final Point. See CR 469.1.a. for
Final Point details.

Deck Checks

At high OPL, judges should perform deck checks during the course of the competition.

When deck checks are performed, at least 10% of all decks, chosen at random, should be
checked during the course of the competition.

Random deck checks should occur at the start of a round, after players have randomized
and presented their decks, but before opening hands are drawn and should verify all
elements of the registered decks.

A deck check can be performed after the mulligan procedure is complete as long as
play has not begun and each player's hand is preserved.

If a deck check is performed within a match, judges should grant a time extension
to that match sufficient to cover the logistical time needed to perform the check.

**505.1.**
**505.2.** Some sequences of actions can be repeated and are shortcutted as a loop.

**505.2.a.** Each iteration of a loop must contain identical, non-conditional actions performed on the
**505.2.a.1.** same set of cards. To establish a loop, the looping player must be able to say with certainty
**505.2.a.2.** exactly what each step of the loop will be, not just the outcome.

505.2.a.2.a.                          Loops must be fully deterministic; they may not rely on probability, decision trees,
or conditional actions. Example:
**505.2.b.**
**505.3.** Legal Loop: With enough Energy available, playing Grim Apothecary to a
**505.3.a.** battlefield then using its trigger to return itself to hand 100 times.
**505.3.b.**
**505.3.c.** Illegal Loop: A card reads "Recycle 3 cards from your trash: Ready a unit." A
**505.4.** player with enough Energy available wants to use this to recycle 2 copies of
**505.5.** Stupefy from their trash and 1 copy of Downstage Dramatics with an

**505.6.** Dramatics from hand, and then play each card as they draw it until there
**505.7.** are 3 cards in the trash for them to recycle.
**505.8.**
This is a non-deterministic loop because the order you play the
cards is not guaranteed. If the loop only included copies of the
same exact card draw spell, it would be deterministic.

Players can manually execute non-deterministic loops, but must do so in a timely
fashion. Failure to do so is Slow Play. See 703.2 for more information on Slow Play.

To decide how to proceed with a loop, determine how many players are needed to maintain
it (who must take actions or make decisions to sustain the loop):

No players

One player

Two or more players

For each maintaining player (if any), in turn order, that player chooses a number of
iterations.

Each other player (if any), in turn order, may choose to agree to that number or choose a
number of iterations after which they commit to taking action to break the loop or choose
not to break the loop.

If no player chooses to break the loop and there were no maintaining players, the game
ends in a draw.

Otherwise, the loop continues until the lowest number of iterations chosen, then that player
either gains priority (if a maintaining player) or breaks the loop.

If a player intervenes to break a loop, they may choose to interrupt a partially completed
iteration.

General

Philosophy

Errors are assumed to be committed unintentionally. If the judge believes it was
intentional, it is cheating. See 704.8 for more information about cheating.

Players attempting to abuse error remedies to receive advantages are
cheating.

Judges are neutral arbiters and enforcers of policy and rules.

Judges should not stop errors from occurring. Instead they deal with errors that
have occurred.

Judges should not intervene in games unless:

They believe a violation has occurred.

A player requested assistance.

Applying Penalties

Penalties of Game Loss of higher (b-d) should be reported to the head judge.

Judges should explain the error, the procedure to fix it, and the penalties for
involved players.

Some errors include remedies beyond the scope of the base penalty, to protect
competition officials from accusations of bias.

If a single error leads to multiple related penalties, judges should issue only the one
with the most severe penalty.

Shuffling

Some remedies require shuffling the randomized portion of the deck.

Many cards recycle cards to the bottom of a deck. The judge should do what they
can using information available from the game state and from the players to
determine which portion of the deck is knowable by one or more players, and
separate those before shuffling that deck.

If no randomized portion of the deck exists and it is possible, judges may
complete remedies without shuffling.

If judges can't confidently determine the full randomized portion of a deck,
they may shuffle as much of the deck as needed to perform a remedy.

Once the deck has been shuffled, any cards set aside in this way should be returned
to their proper locations in the deck.

play for final rankings.

**704.6.** agree to drop from the tournament. Prizes are then awarded based on each

Conduct Rules

General Obligations: Players who participate in Riftbound competitions must at all
times observe the highest standards of personal integrity and sporting conduct.
Players are required to behave in a respectful manner in all of their interactions
with other competitors, judges, competition officials, the media, sponsors, fans and
other persons. Players must promptly comply with the decisions and instructions of
Competition Officials (particularly of Judges) in a respectful and non-combative
manner.

Unsporting Conduct. Players may not engage in any unsporting conduct listed in
**704.** Engaging in unsporting conduct may additionally be subject to sanction under
this Disciplinary Code.

Cheating. Cheating is prohibited. Enabling or assisting others to cheat is also
prohibited.

Unauthorized Communications. Unauthorized coaching and other
communications during a match can give a player an unfair competitive
advantage, and are prohibited.

Derogatory or Defamatory Statements and Hateful Acts. Players may not use
words, gestures, digital or physical objects, or any other means to transmit, allow or
facilitate messages or actions that insult, encourage hatred, undermine, or harm
the integrity of a country, individual, or group of people. This includes
contemptuous, discriminatory, or denigrating expressions based on race, skin color,
ethnic, national, or social origin, gender, language, religion, political or other
opinions, financial status, birth, sexual orientation, medical condition or history, or
any other characteristic or status.

Vulgar or Provocative. Players may not use words, gestures, digital or physical
objects, or any other means to transmit, allow or facilitate messages or actions that
**705.3.g.** are obscene, vulgar, provocative, hateful, racist, insulting, threatening, abusive,
**705.3.h.** libelous, slanderous, defamatory, or otherwise offensive or objectionable.
**705.3.i.**
**705.3.j.** Harassment. Players may not engage in any form of harassment. Harassment is
defined as unwelcome and unwanted behaviors that diminish, demean, or
**705.3.k.** otherwise intimidate. This rule also prohibits sexual harassment, which is defined as
**705.3.l.** any form of unwelcome sexual advances. Stalking, conduct that involves threats of
violence (whether physically or online) and sharing personal information of another
individual without permission are considered forms of harassment as is any
conduct by an individual that incites or encourages abuse by others. All forms of
harassment are prohibited, even if the behavior is not sufficiently severe or
pervasive to constitute unlawful activity under applicable law.

Bullying and Abuse. Players may not engage in any form of bullying or physical or
mental abuse. Bullying is defined as malicious conduct which an objective observer
would consider hostile, offensive, and unrelated to legitimate competition,
especially in circumstances where the person is perceived as vulnerable. Abuse is
defined as treating a person with cruelty or violence (including physical
altercation), especially if done repeatedly. Acting with reckless disregard for the
potential negative impact of one's actions is also considered a form of bullying and
is prohibited.

Credible Threats. Players may not threaten others. A threat is defined as a
statement of intention to commit an act of violence or take other hostile action
against someone in retribution for something done or not done that could cause an
objective observer to believe it will be carried out.

Match-fixing or Manipulation. Players may not match-fix or otherwise manipulate
the outcome of any match or competition or request, encourage, or instruct others
to manipulate it. Manipulation is defined as the unlawful or undue influence or
alteration, directly or indirectly, by an act or an omission, of the course, result, or
any other aspect of a match (i.e. spot-fixing) or a competition, irrespective of
whether the behavior is committed for financial gain, sporting advantage, or any
other purpose. For the purposes of this rule, manipulation also includes any attempt
to manipulate, any action that creates the appearance of manipulation, and/or any
behavior that could reasonably give rise to suspicions of manipulation, whether or
not the manipulation is ultimately carried out or successful.

Retaliation. Players may not retaliate against anyone who reports a possible
violation of these Riftbound Tournament Rules in good faith or who provides
truthful information during an investigation. Retaliation (for example, threats,
intimidation, harassment, employment actions, or any other form of reprisal)
constitutes an independent violation, regardless of the outcome of the underlying
report.

Violations of Law. Players are required to comply with all applicable laws at all
times during their participation in Riftbound competitions. Players may not engage
in (or encourage or solicit others to engage in) any illegal activity or behavior that
is under investigation or has resulted in formal charges, whether or not a conviction
has occurred, if such activity may bring Riot Games, the Competition Organizer,
and/or a Riftbound competition into disrepute, negatively affect the authority,
honor, and brand value of Riot Games, the Competition Organizer, and/or a
Riftbound competition, or cause harm to the operation of a Riftbound competition.
**705.3.m.** Violations of Riot Games Terms of Service. Players are required to comply with
the Riot Games Terms of Service and may face sanctions under this Disciplinary
Code in addition to consequences established in the Riot Games Terms of Service.
